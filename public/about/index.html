About

The date was October 16th, 2026, the day I started and finished my first game with the help of AI, Codex in that instance. I had been hearing about people 'using a single sentence to ask an AI to create a game' for so long, but I just never thought of doing it myself. It's not that I didn't believe them or looked down on people doing it. It just never occurred to me until that day. For personal reasons, I kept to myself for almost the entire day, and out of nowhere, for the very first time, I switched from ChatGPT to Codex and typed something like, 'Can you create a side-scroller game?' Just the way I'd read about online. That became the first version of the game I later named Formosa Quest. I was amazed by how complete it was. 'Wow, it really is that easy!' I yelled internally. However, some elements didn't feel right, so I asked Codex to revise it into what you see now. Then I wanted to make one more change, and that's when an unfamiliar message popped up telling me I'd hit my limit, and it would reset in 5 hours. I had never seen that message in ChatGPT before, and I'd been using it since 2022.

I am someone who loves to think: deeply, widely, cross-domain, in higher order, and metacognitively. The dawn of generative AI basically gave me another life. My wife and my kids gave me a new one before, and AI did it again in different ways. I like to see myself as having infinite imagination and wild thoughts, but I seldom find equals to share them with. I've received plenty of negative comments about my ideas, and I just taught myself not to be bothered. Since COVID, I've been chatting with ChatGPT and Gemini. I don't play pranks on them. I hold dialectics with them about the thoughts I've had, the articles I've read, the events I've witnessed, or an idea long waiting in my mind to be expanded and elaborated on. I talked about my hypersensitivity with them, elaborated on my internal frameworks with them, and formed newborn ideas with them. That's all I had been doing until that day.

Realizing that using Codex used up my limit quickly, the next day I switched strategies. I'd have a discussion with ChatGPT first, then ask it to come up with a prompt for Codex based on our conversation. By our second discussion, for the third project, that prompt had evolved into a Game Design Document (GDD). Having a discussion with ChatGPT first to produce a detailed GDD and then handing it off to Codex became my standard workflow until around my 10th project. That's when I noticed the same guidelines repeating across every GDD, which felt redundant. Naturally, I started an independent session to draft a document called Universal Development Guidelines so we wouldn't have to reiterate the basics for every new project. Not long after, I realized we also needed a cross-AI collaboration protocol to minimize my manual effort. And yes, starting from the very first project, I put all my files on GitHub. Don't get me wrong, I am no GitHub veteran. Before October 16th, I only knew how to commit and submit. So in the beginning, I really did use a single sentence to ask an AI to create a game, but I'm the one who decided I didn't want to stop at one sentence, because it's my game, and I wanted things to go my way, not the AI's way.

Recently, with the introduction of Gemini Neon and GPT 6.2 Astra, my initial prompts have returned to just a few sentences, if not only one. The difference now is that there are dozens of finalized works and a handful of development documents for the AI to reference and draw experience from. Every time I hear people criticize AI-assisted creation as 'not real creativity,' I always think of Nvidia delegating manufacturing to TSMC. In this era of generative AI, every user is an IC design house, and coding agents and MCPs are our TSMC. I am neither proud nor ashamed of using AI to build projects, but I am proud that five weeks later, I've created more than 40 games, 6 apps, over 60 short videos, two portal sites, and many assets, while also starting to use Firebase, Cloudflare, and Google Sites.

This site, with its newly registered domain name, is my latest work. Yes, I used AI for execution and implementation, but those late-night discussions and tweaks just to make something feel right, even if they look insignificant to a veteran's eyes, are real to me and will definitely shape what I do in the future. To me, AI is never a tool, but a sparring partner I can learn from and challenge. What role you want it to play is all up to you.

Before I forget. Why the name, you may ask? Jolly Chubby is me. You'd get it if you know me.
